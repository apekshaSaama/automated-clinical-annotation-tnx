A 59-year-old male with a past medical history of tobacco and marijuana use, chronic back pain not on narcotics, and untreated hypertension and dyslipidemia was transferred from an outside hospital for further workup after presenting there with acute substernal chest pain radiating to his anterior neck. He was found to be hypotensive and bradycardic with a blood pressure of 70s/40s and a heart rate in the 40s. He was given atropine in route and on arrival received a normal saline bolus, morphine, ondansetron, and lorazepam for his symptoms. The electrocardiogram showed sinus bradycardia with no ST-segment elevations. Chest radiography showed a mildly enlarged cardiac silhouette with no acute abnormalities. Labs were significant for a negative troponin and an elevated D dimer to 35 mg/L FEU. He was transferred to our facility because their CT scan was not functioning.
On arrival, the patient had a blood pressure of 114/45 with a heart rate of 54 bpm. He continued to have constant chest pain. On physical exam, he had normal heart sounds and equal radial and femoral pulses. His blood pressure could not initially be obtained on the right upper extremity, but after being taken manually was similar to the left. His electrocardiogram showed low voltage sinus bradycardia, and so a bedside transthoracic echo (TTE) was done.
